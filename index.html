SETLOG Social — Terms & Community Rules

Effective 10 August 2026 · Operated from the United Kingdom by the SETLOG developer.

1. What this is

SETLOG is a workout tracker. SETLOG Social is its optional, opt-in social layer: you can publish a workout as a post (with your progress photo from that workout), follow people, comment, and like. Your training data lives on your own phone and is never uploaded unless you choose to share a specific workout. These terms cover the social layer; by joining it in the app you accept them.

A post can also say which gym the workout was at — “at Gym Unity · Lichfield”, on the post itself. A gym is always named in full, chain and branch together, exactly as it is written in SETLOG. It comes from the workout, not from anything you fill in when you post: it is the gym recorded on that workout, which is yours to set and to change on the workout screen while you train, and it can only ever be a gym you had joined — a workout you logged somewhere you were only visiting carries no gym name. There is no separate switch for it, so who sees the gym’s name is decided by the audience you choose for the post, the same as everything else on it. The name shows while that gym has a SETLOG subscription; if a gym stops subscribing, its name stops appearing on posts — older ones as well as new — and nothing on the post is changed or deleted, so the name comes back if the gym subscribes again. Posts from anyone under 18 never carry a gym name at all, whatever their gym does. That is settled when the post is made: turning 18 later does not add a gym name to posts made before then.

If your gym is set up on SETLOG, two more things can happen. The gym can post notices to the people whose main gym is that branch (“closing early today”), which can arrive as a notification on your phone. And the gym can have leaderboards: you record a short video of a lift, enter the weight, and your lift is ranked against other people at that branch. Only members of that branch can see that branch’s boards and videos. Taking part is entirely up to you. If your gym is on SETLOG, its staff build the list of equipment at that branch, so what you log matches what is actually in the room. The gym can also post updates on its own page — a new machine arriving, a rule change — as a short piece of text with an optional photo or video. Updates are seen only by that branch’s members, and they stay on the gym’s page until the gym takes them down. A new update also appears in your feed alongside posts from people at your gym, for the same first week that their posts do, and then leaves the feed while staying on the gym’s page. Updates are switched off in the app at the moment. No gym can post one and no member sees one anywhere; nothing already posted has been deleted, and what is described just above is what returns when they are switched back on.

Some of this depends on the gym. Notices and the notifications that carry them, leaderboards, posting a workout to your gym in the feed, and the gym’s name showing on a post all depend on the gym’s own SETLOG subscription: where a gym does not have one, those parts are simply not there — for its members and for its own staff alike. If you had already put an entry on that gym’s board, you keep a list of your own entries there and can delete them; what goes is the board itself, everyone else’s entries on it, and the ability to add another. A gym’s page and its equipment list are free for every gym, so what you can look up about a gym never depends on that. Nothing is deleted if a gym stops subscribing — its notices, its boards, the entries and videos on them and the workouts posted to it are all kept, and a post that named the gym still holds that record with only the name hidden; they all come back if it subscribes again. One thing to know about that: the ordinary rules in section 5 about how long leaderboard videos and post photos are kept carry on running the whole time, exactly as they would have done, so over a long gap some videos and photos will have reached the end of those rules and gone. Nothing is removed because a gym stopped subscribing, and nothing is kept longer because it did.

Every gym on SETLOG has a page, and anyone signed in can look at it whether or not they have joined that gym. The gym’s owner writes what is on it: a logo, a short description of the gym, its opening hours, its address, links to its own website and Instagram, and a list of what it has — free weights, a sauna, parking, and so on — next to the equipment list described above. A gym’s page is written by the gym itself and is not pre-screened, in the same way its updates and its equipment names are not. Tapping the address, the website or the Instagram link takes you out of SETLOG to somewhere our rules do not reach. There is no Report button on a gym’s page — if something on one is wrong, or should not be there, email setlogmobile@outlook.com and we will deal with it.

You will also be able to message a gym privately. It is a one-to-one conversation between you and that gym — no other member can see it, and it is not a post, a comment or anything that appears on a page (our own moderators can read it, and that is set out below). A number of things are worth knowing before you use it, and they are all deliberate. A gym can never message you first: it can only reply inside a conversation you started, which is built into SETLOG rather than being a setting anyone can change. Messages are text only — there is no way to send a photo, a video or a file. Everything sent, in both directions, is checked automatically before the other person sees it, including the gym’s replies; until a message has been checked, the only people who can see it are whoever wrote it and our moderators. Our moderators can read messages — that is how a reported one gets dealt with, and it is worth saying plainly about a private conversation even though the same is true of everything else on SETLOG. You can report anything a gym sends you, and one report is enough to hide it while one of our moderators looks — everywhere else on SETLOG that takes three different people. And you can delete your own messages at any time; the gym can delete its own too, and neither side can edit anything once it has been sent. You cannot block a gym, because blocks are for people rather than organisations. A gym can close a conversation, which stops you sending anything further in it — but it does not stop the gym, and that asymmetry is worth stating plainly: a closed conversation can still receive the gym’s replies. Nothing already there is deleted, you can still report anything in it, and once none of your own messages is left in it the gym cannot write there either. Messaging a gym needs a SETLOG Social account, but not a membership of that gym, and it is one of the parts that depend on the gym’s own SETLOG subscription — at a gym without one there is no way to message it, and email remains the way to reach us about anything to do with that gym. Messaging is not in the app yet. Nothing described here can be done at the moment; it is set out in full because these are the rules that apply from the moment it appears.

2. The rules — zero tolerance

There is zero tolerance for objectionable content or abusive behaviour. That includes, without limitation:

  • harassment, bullying, threats, or hate directed at anyone;
  • nudity, sexual content, gore, or violent content;
  • illegal content of any kind;
  • spam, scams, or impersonation;
  • posting other people's private information.
  • filming other people at your gym. A leaderboard clip is of you lifting — frame it so other members are not the subject, and never post a clip of someone who has asked you not to.
  • faking a lift — edited or misrepresented video, or a weight you did not lift.

Content that breaks these rules is removed, and accounts that keep breaking them lose access to the social layer. You must be 13 or older to join, and 18 or older to post publicly.

3. Moderation

Posts, photos and comments are checked before anyone else sees them. Every one starts hidden — only its author can see it while it waits — and is checked automatically within seconds of being posted. If the check objects to it, or if it cannot run, the item stays hidden until a human moderator looks at it.

Leaderboard entries are different, and you should know it before you take part. An entry and its video appear on your gym’s board straight away, with nothing checking it first — a board that lags behind the lift is not a board. What protects people instead: only members of that gym branch can see it; if three different people report an entry it is hidden automatically, in seconds, before any human looks; and the gym’s own staff then decide whether to put it back up or take it down, with SETLOG able to overrule them either way. Where a gym does not have a subscription its boards are not shown to its members at all, and in that case SETLOG’s own moderators make that decision instead of its staff — so a reported entry can still be seen by them. If this turns out to work badly, we can switch entries to being checked first, or switch the boards off entirely, without needing an app update.

Equipment names written by gyms are different again. A gym’s verified staff write them, they appear straight away, and they are not put through the automatic check — they are short factual names of machines, written by people whose identity we have checked. Anyone can report one. Because the gym itself wrote it, it is SETLOG’s moderator who decides, not the gym.

A gym’s updates work the same way — these rules are set out in full because they apply the moment updates are switched back on, which section 1 explains they are not at present — and you should know what that means, because they are more than a machine’s name: an update can carry free text and a photo or a video. They are written only by the gym’s verified staff, they appear straight away, and they are not put through the automatic check that members’ own posts go through. What protects people instead is the same as elsewhere: anyone at that branch can report an update; reports from three different people hide it automatically, in seconds, before any human looks; and because the gym itself wrote it, it is SETLOG’s moderator who decides whether it comes back — not the gym. An update cannot be edited after it is posted; a gym corrects one by taking it down and posting again.

Private messages between you and a gym are different again, and in the opposite direction from everything above: they are checked more, not less. Every message is checked automatically before the other person can see it — the gym’s replies as well as yours — which is not true of a gym’s notices, its updates or its equipment names. Until a message has been checked, the only people who can see it are whoever wrote it and our moderators, and if the check cannot run it stays that way rather than going through. Because a conversation has only two people in it, there is no wider audience who could report something in it, so one report is enough to hide a message rather than the three needed everywhere else. A hidden message is hidden, not deleted, and one of our moderators decides. Neither side can edit a message; either side can delete its own outright, and deleting yours also stops the gym replying. See section 1 for what messaging is and section 5 for what happens to it when you leave.

Content that other users report is hidden automatically once reports from three different people are open at the same time, before any human looks at it — with private messages to a gym the one exception, where one report is enough, for the reason given just above. Reports are private: the person who is reported is never told who reported them, and neither is their gym. A human makes the final call — approve, or reject. Rejected content stays visible only to its author and is never shown to anyone else.

4. Reporting, complaints, and appeals

Every post, photo, comment and profile carries a Report action in the app, and you can block any account (blocking is mutual invisibility). Use these first — they are the fastest route.

For anything else — including a complaint that your own content was wrongly removed or your report was wrongly dismissed — contact: setlogmobile@outlook.com. Complaints are acknowledged within 72 hours and decided within 7 days. If you are in the UK and believe content is illegal, you can also report it to the police.

5. Your content and leaving

A shared workout is a snapshot made at the moment you post it, and it is fixed from that moment: a post cannot be edited afterwards, so changing anything means deleting it and sharing again. You can delete your own posts and comments at any time. Deleting your own leaderboard entries does not depend on the gym subscribing: at any gym you have joined, the app shows you a list of every entry you have made there — including the older, lighter ones a board keeps as history behind your best — with a delete button on each of them, whether or not that gym subscribes. If you leave a gym, the entries you made there are kept — leaving does not take them off that gym’s board, though reports and moderation still can, in the ways section 3 describes — and while you are away no control in the app leads you to that delete: rejoining that gym brings that list and its delete buttons back, and leaving the social layer altogether removes them along with everything else you posted (last paragraph of this section).

Leaderboard videos are not kept forever. A video is kept while your entry is in the top few on its board. If it drops out, the video is kept for another seven days and is then deleted from SETLOG — your place on the board, your weight and your name all stay. If your entry never gets into the top few at all, those seven days run from the day you put it on the board. Climbing back into the top few stops that clock, though not once a deletion has already begun. When you film a clip, we also ask to save a copy to your own phone’s camera roll. Allow that, and it stays yours even after ours is deleted — deleting a leaderboard video only ever removes our copy, never yours. An entry that has been reported is an exception: its video is kept, because we have to be able to look at it.

Post photos are not kept forever either. Your post stays in the feeds for seven days. After that it drops out of them, and the photograph on it is deleted from SETLOG. The post itself is not deleted: its title, the workout, your numbers, the likes and every comment stay, and you can still open it from that workout in your own history. The photo you post is the progress photo you took for that workout, and that copy stays on your phone — on your calendar — so deleting ours takes nothing away from you. A post that has been reported is an exception: its photo is kept, for the same reason.

Leaving the social layer (Settings → SETLOG Social) permanently deletes everything you posted — posts, photos, comments, likes, follows, your leaderboard entries and the videos behind them, and your social profile — from our servers. Your private conversations with gyms go too, and they go whole — your messages and the gym’s replies to you, so nothing is left on the gym’s side that you cannot see. There is no separate step for it and nothing is kept back. Your workout history on your phone is untouched.

Nothing deletes your messages on a schedule. The only two things SETLOG deletes on a timer are leaderboard videos and post photos, on the schedules described above. A message is not one of them — and neither are your posts, your comments or a gym’s own updates, which we also keep. What is unusual about a message is the two facts together: nobody sees it but you, that gym and our moderators, and nothing expires it. It stays until you delete it, or the gym deletes its own, or you leave the social layer — and then it is gone rather than hidden.

6. Privacy, briefly

  • Profiles are private by default; you choose to make yours public.
  • Public posts show the day a workout happened, never the time.
  • Leaderboard videos are stored privately and are only ever shown to members of that gym branch — never publicly, and never on the web.
  • Training data stays on your device unless you share a workout.
  • Sign-in is by email code; we store your email address to run your account, and the content you choose to publish. Nothing is sold or shared with advertisers.
